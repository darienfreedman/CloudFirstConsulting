Threat protection and data security

Defender for Cloud

The problem: Cloud teams struggle to identify exposed workloads, prioritize configuration risks, and maintain consistent protection across environments.

Combine cloud security posture management with workload protection across supported Azure, connected multicloud, and hybrid resources. Select plans, connect environments, and establish protection coverage around workload risk and operational requirements.

  • Posture management: assess recommendations, vulnerabilities, and supported attack paths to prioritize exposure by business impact. Assign remediation owners and track exceptions rather than treating a score as proof of security.
  • Workload protection: enable relevant plans for servers, containers, databases, storage, and other supported services. Confirm connector, agent, permission, and pricing requirements before onboarding.
  • Operational response: route relevant alerts into security investigations and response workflows. Validate integrations, escalation responsibilities, and protection coverage as workloads change.
Explore related consultingRead official documentationPlan cloud security posture managementReview workload protection plans

Defender XDR

The problem: Disconnected security signals obscure attacks spanning email, endpoints, identities, and cloud applications.

Unify protection across email, endpoints, identities, and cloud applications. Extended detection and response (XDR) connects signals into incidents for coordinated investigation and response.

  • Automatic attack disruption: contain compromised assets during high-confidence attacks to limit lateral movement and damage while analysts investigate.
  • Predictive shielding: use supported endpoint protections to anticipate attacker actions, harden exposed paths, and proactively restrict high-risk users before an incident escalates.
  • Operational control: validate licensing, onboarding, exclusions, and supported actions. Review automated decisions and recovery procedures with security and service owners.
  • Security for AI: coordinate threat detection, identity controls, data protection, and governance around supported AI workloads.
Explore related consultingRead official documentationUnderstand attack disruption and predictive shieldingExplore security for AI

Entra

The problem: Organizations need to control who can access applications and information without granting unnecessary network access.

Entra combines identity and access capabilities. Plan identity lifecycle, sign-in risk, application access, and secure connectivity together rather than treating them as separate permission systems. Apply Zero Trust principles: verify explicitly, use least-privilege access, and assume breach when designing controls.

  • Entra ID manages identities, authentication, and application access; ID means identity in these product names.
  • Entra ID Governance supports access reviews and lifecycle processes. Entra ID Protection identifies identity risk. Conditional Access applies access decisions using configured signals and policies.
  • Entra Private Access provides Zero Trust Network Access for supported private applications. Entra Internet Access provides a secure web gateway for internet and cloud-service traffic.
  • Entra Suite brings capabilities together under a licensing offer. Validate prerequisites and replace eligible virtual private network access in stages, not as an assumption that every network connection can be removed.

Purview

The problem: Sensitive information is difficult to discover, classify, protect, and govern across its lifecycle.

Connect data classification, information protection, data loss prevention, and supported compliance capabilities to business handling requirements. Licensing and workload support determine the available controls.

  • Discovery and governance: identify data owners, locate sensitive information, and use catalog and lineage context to guide decisions about trust, access, and reuse across supported sources.
  • Information protection: define classifications and sensitivity labels, then apply data loss prevention controls to supported collaboration and data movement. Test policies against real work before broader enforcement.
  • Compliance and investigations: plan retention, audit, eDiscovery, and insider risk capabilities with legal and privacy owners. Product configuration supports those processes; it does not establish compliance by itself.
  • AI readiness: review oversharing and how assistants use protected information. Agree evidence, exceptions, and ownership, then prioritize improvements rather than assuming every connected application enforces identical controls.
