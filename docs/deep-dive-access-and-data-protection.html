Technology deep dive / Access and data protection

Limit access and protect information.

Least privilege limits a person, application, or agent to the access needed for an approved task. Data loss prevention uses policies to identify and help control inappropriate handling or sharing of sensitive information.

Distinguish the enforcement layers.

Identity controls evaluate who is requesting access and under what conditions. Resource permissions determine which files, records, or applications that identity can reach. Information-protection controls can apply classification, encryption, or handling restrictions where the workload supports them.

These layers answer different questions. A user may be allowed to read a document but not to share it externally. A compliant device does not automatically justify access to every repository. A label can express a handling requirement without every connected application enforcing it in the same way.

Understand how policies become actions.

A data loss prevention policy typically combines a scope, matching conditions, and supported actions. Depending on the workload, it may audit, warn, require justification, or block an operation. The exact behavior depends on configuration, licensing, and the service processing the information.

Classification helps determine what needs protection, but false positives and missed matches remain possible. Policies need representative examples, understandable user guidance, and ownership for exceptions rather than relying on a label or rule name as proof of effectiveness.

Example: sharing a project report.

An employee can access a report through an approved project group. A sharing policy may still restrict external distribution because the report contains sensitive information. When an assistant summarizes the report, its retrieval must respect access and its supported handling path must be assessed separately.

If the summary is copied into another application, the original controls may not behave identically. Trace where the information moves, which classification context is preserved, and which component can enforce the intended restriction.

Validate the effective boundary.

Test authorized and denied access, group changes, external sharing, and supported AI interactions. Review broad permissions, service identities, and exceptions that outlast their purpose. Combine access evidence with policy results and user impact. Technical controls support security and compliance processes, but do not replace legal interpretation, risk acceptance, or accountable data ownership.
