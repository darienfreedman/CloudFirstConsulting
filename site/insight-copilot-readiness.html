Perspective / Data security

Before you scale Copilot,
revisit who can see what.

September 21, 2026

Artificial intelligence (AI) adoption changes how easily people can find and use organizational knowledge. It does not make broad access permissions a sound business decision. A useful readiness conversation starts with the information a use case needs and the people who should be able to reach it.

Separate the Copilot experience from the model.

Microsoft 365 Copilot is not a single generative pre-trained transformer model. It combines models with orchestration, grounding in work information, application integrations, and permission-aware access. Model choices can evolve without removing the need for access controls, data protection, and human accountability. Supported choices differ between Copilot products and features; this is not unrestricted access to any model.

If an early experience fell short, evaluate the current product and configuration against your own tasks. Compare answer quality, source relevance, and review effort instead of assuming a model change fixes every problem. Read the plain-language guide to Copilot, models, and governance.

Start with a bounded use case.

Choose a team, a set of tasks, and a manageable collection of information. Identify a business owner and define what useful output looks like. A focused pilot makes it easier to evaluate value and spot information-handling problems before expanding.

Review access before adding complexity.

Look for broadly shared sites, stale membership, guest access, and content without a clear owner. Prioritize repositories relevant to the pilot rather than attempting to repair the entire estate at once. Record why access should change and how legitimate collaboration will continue.

Make protection usable.

Classification and labeling should reflect business handling requirements. Data loss prevention policies need an understood purpose, a staged rollout, and a way to manage exceptions. Product capabilities vary by workload and licensing; confirm support rather than assuming every interaction is covered.

Evaluate usefulness and boundaries together.

Test whether the solution helps with the intended tasks, grounds responses appropriately, and respects the access model. Include questions the system should not answer. A fluent response is not, by itself, evidence of a correct or authorized response.

Make the next decision explicit.

At the end of a pilot, review readiness gaps, user feedback, quality, and remaining risks. Decide whether to expand, refine, or pause. Assign owners to the next actions so that adoption does not outrun the controls needed to sustain it.

Make adoption part of the workplace.

Use Microsoft 365 and Viva to support the people behind the rollout. Viva Connections can surface approved guidance, Engage can support peer communities, and Learning can make role-based training easier to find. Where Viva Insights is appropriate, agree privacy safeguards and measurement purposes first. Combine feedback and task quality with usage information rather than assuming activity equals value.

Validate the supported behavior for your environment.
